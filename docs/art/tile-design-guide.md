# Conduit Tile and Board Art Guide

**Project:** Pathweaver: Pocket Realms
**Audience:** 2D artist. No programming or Unity knowledge assumed.
**Status of the art:** nothing exists yet. Every shape currently on screen is
generated by code at runtime as flat coloured geometry, so this document is the
brief rather than a description of something to match.

## How to read this document

The project has two sources of truth: `PRD.md`, which is the product spec, and
the game code, which is what actually runs. Where this document reports what one
of those says, it says so. Where it proposes something that nobody has decided
yet, it is labelled **Proposal**. Proposals are arguable — if you have a better
answer, the reasoning is given so you can attack it. Anything labelled
**Established** is not arguable without changing the game.

---

## 1. The world you are drawing

### Established by the PRD

Pathweaver is a **cozy, tactile route-building puzzle game** for Android phones,
played offline with one thumb. The two players it is designed for are the daily
commuter who wants a self-contained puzzle on a train, and the "Cozy Minimalist"
who is there for calm ambient art and a low-stress difficulty curve. There are
**no timers and no reflex pressure anywhere in the game** — a player can put the
phone in a pocket mid-thought and come back an hour later. The art has to carry
that: nothing urgent, nothing alarming, nothing that pulses for attention.

The fiction, as far as the PRD commits to it:

- The player routes **resources from source springs to destination hubs** by
  laying hexagonal conduit tiles on a grid.
- There are four resources: **Water, Wind, Crystal, Trade**.
- Completing a route makes resources **flow through the line** and pay out.
- The harvested currency is **Star Essence**, and it unlocks adjacent nodes on
  the **World Atlas** — a *non-linear constellation map*, not a level ladder.
  Progression is horizontal: new regions dock onto the outer edge of the
  constellation.
- The campaign is **120+ handcrafted stages across diverse biomes**, each biome
  introducing distinct environmental rules. The PRD's examples of such rules are
  **frozen rivers** and **volcanic vents**. Note the precise wording: those are
  named as examples of *mechanics*, not as a confirmed list of biome themes. They
  are strong hints about art direction, not a locked-in brief.
- The first three biomes ship free. Later biomes arrive as regional packs.
- A paid **Supporter Pass** (post-launch) adds **five exclusive hand-painted tile
  themes** and **golden path particle trails**.

### What is open for you to invent

Almost all of it. The PRD gives nouns and a tone; it does not describe what a
spring looks like, what a hub looks like, what a conduit is made of, whether the
world is seen from above or at a slight angle, or who built the network. Nobody
has decided whether the conduits are aqueducts, root systems, ley lines, rails,
or something stranger. That is genuinely yours.

Two pieces of framing that may help you design with intent rather than
decorating a grid:

- The player is a **router**, not a builder or a fighter. Every satisfying moment
  in this game is the moment a line finally connects and something moves along
  it. Art that sells "connection" and "flow" is doing the core job. Art that
  sells "construction" or "conquest" is fighting the design.
- Long routes are worth exponentially more than short ones, so the game
  constantly tempts players into sprawling circuits that risk deadlocking the
  board. A completed long route should feel like a small triumph of tidiness.
  That is the emotional peak the art is building toward.

### Please avoid

- Anything hostile, gory, or high-alarm. There is no combat and no failure
  spectacle.
- Baked-in text or numerals anywhere in the artwork. The game will be localised,
  and text in a texture cannot be translated.
- Visual noise that implies time pressure: flashing warnings, countdown motifs,
  crumbling or burning states.

## 2. What is on screen today, and why it is not a reference

For context only. The current build draws:

- Each cell as a flat hexagon mesh generated at **92% of the true cell radius**,
  which is why there is a visible gutter between cells. The true cell footprint
  is the full radius; the gutter is a placeholder choice, not a rule.
- Each open edge as a plain rectangular **spoke** running from the tile centre
  out to the edge midpoint, rotated into place. One rectangle mesh serves all six
  directions.
- Springs, hubs, buttons and the progress bar as flat coloured shapes.

The spokes are drawn slightly *longer* than the shrunken hexagon, so they
currently poke past the tile's visible edge. That is what makes neighbouring
tiles look joined despite the gutter.

Treat none of this as an aesthetic target. It exists so that the game could be
built and tested without waiting on an artist. What you *should* take from it is
the geometry, which is exact and which real art has to honour.

## 3. The hexagon: exact geometry

**Established in code** (`Assets/Scripts/Presentation/HexMetrics.cs`,
`HexMeshFactory.cs`).

The board uses **pointy-top hexagons**: a single corner at the top, a single
corner at the bottom, and **flat vertical edges on the left and right**. This
orientation was chosen because rows stack more tightly vertically than columns do
horizontally, so more of the board fits above the thumb on a portrait phone. The
game is **portrait-locked** — it never rotates to landscape.

"World units" below are Unity's internal length unit. They are not pixels; the
conversion to pixels is in section 10.

| Measurement | Value in world units | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Centre to corner (the radius) | **0.5** | Set by `HexMetrics.Size`. |
| Corner to corner, vertically | **1.0** | Twice the radius. This is the tile's full height. |
| Centre to edge midpoint (the apothem) | **0.4330** | Exactly `0.5 × √3 ÷ 2`. |
| Flat edge to flat edge, horizontally | **0.8660** | Exactly `0.5 × √3`. This is the tile's full width. |
| Centre to centre between neighbours | **0.8660** | Exactly `0.5 × √3`. Same in all six directions. |

The important consequence: **centre-to-centre spacing equals the flat-to-flat
width**, which means neighbouring tiles touch exactly, edge against edge, with no
overlap and no gap. Whatever you paint at the edge of one tile sits directly
against whatever the neighbouring tile paints at its matching edge.

A tile is **taller than it is wide**, by a factor of `1 ÷ 0.866 = 1.1547`. Do not
draw it as if it fitted a square: a regular pointy-top hexagon of height H has
width `0.866 × H`.

## 4. Edge numbering

**Established in code** (`src/Pathweaver.Core/Hex/HexCoord.cs`,
`src/Pathweaver.Core/Tiles/EdgeMask.cs`), and derived here by working out where
each index lands on screen.

Every tile has six edges, numbered **0 to 5**. The game stores which edges a tile
is open on as a set of these numbers. When a filename or a level file says
`water 0,3`, it means a water tile open on edges 0 and 3.

The numbers run **clockwise on screen, starting from due east (the right-hand
vertical edge)**:

| Edge index | Compass direction on screen | Which edge that is | Offset from the tile centre to that edge's midpoint (world units) | Opposite edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | East | The vertical edge on the right | x +0.4330, y 0 | 3 |
| **1** | South-east | The lower-right slanted edge | x +0.2165, y −0.3750 | 4 |
| **2** | South-west | The lower-left slanted edge | x −0.2165, y −0.3750 | 5 |
| **3** | West | The vertical edge on the left | x −0.4330, y 0 | 0 |
| **4** | North-west | The upper-left slanted edge | x −0.2165, y +0.3750 | 1 |
| **5** | North-east | The upper-right slanted edge | x +0.2165, y +0.3750 | 2 |

**Opposite edges are always `index + 3`, wrapping around at 6.** So 0 faces 3, 1
faces 4, 2 faces 5. This is why a conduit leaving one tile through edge 0 must
meet its neighbour's edge 3.

### If you find a compass label that disagrees with this table

Trust the table. It was derived from the rendering code rather than from any
comment.

This is worth stating because the labels were wrong until recently. The header
comment in `levels/biome1-01.pwlevel` and several test constants named index 1
"north-east" and index 4 "south-west", which is the textbook mapping and is
vertically flipped from what renders. The rendering code deliberately negates the
vertical axis, which turns that ordering into the clockwise on-screen ordering
above. Both have since been corrected, and nothing ever behaved wrongly because
the game is driven by the numbers rather than the words — but an artist trusting
those words would have mirrored every slanted edge.

`Assets/Scripts/Presentation/HexMetrics.cs` is the source of truth. If a comment
anywhere disagrees with it, the comment is the thing that is wrong.

## 5. Rotation: the single hardest constraint

**Established in code** (`EdgeMask.RotateClockwise`,
`ConduitTile.RotateClockwise`).

A tile's set of open edges is stored as six bits, and rotating the tile is
literally rotating those six bits by one position. Players rotate the tile in
hand freely before placing it, in **60-degree steps, clockwise**.

This means: **you draw each tile once, in one orientation, and the game turns the
same image to all six positions.** There is no separate drawing per rotation, and
adding one is not on the table — it would multiply the texture budget by six.

Consequences you must design around:

- **No baked drop shadows.** A shadow that falls to the lower-right will fall to
  the upper-left when the tile is rotated 180 degrees, and the board will look
  like it is lit from six directions at once.
- **No baked directional lighting.** No highlight along the top edge, no
  darkening along the bottom. If the tile needs to feel three-dimensional, the
  shading must be **radially symmetric** about the tile centre, or applied per
  edge in a way that is identical on all six edges.
- **Nothing that reads as upside-down when turned.** No faces, no letters, no
  arrows, no gravity-dependent detail like drips, flags or plants growing upward.
  Water in a channel cannot be drawn with a surface line, because "up" changes.
- **Six-fold or centre-symmetric decoration is safest.** A motif that is the same
  under any 60-degree turn is unconditionally safe. A motif with no orientation
  at all — a texture, a stipple, a radial gradient — is also safe.

The game *may* later add whole-board lighting through the URP 2D renderer, which
would produce a consistent light direction across the entire board at runtime.
That is the correct place for directional light. It is not your job to paint it
in.

## 6. Tile anatomy: the handshake at the edge

### Established rules

- A conduit tile has **at least two open edges**. A tile with one opening is a
  dead end and the game refuses to create it (`ConduitTile` throws). A tile with
  none is a blank. You will never need to draw either.
- Two conduits connect **only** when this tile is open on the edge facing the
  neighbour, the neighbour is open on the opposite edge, and **both carry the
  same resource**. Kinds never interconnect, even when the geometry lines up.
- Openings meet **exactly at edge midpoints** — see the offsets in section 4.
  Because tiles butt up flush, the conduit painted on one tile must arrive at
  precisely the same point, at precisely the same width, as the conduit on the
  tile next door.

### The handshake specification

**Proposal.** The numbers below are mine, chosen to match the placeholder
geometry the game already uses so the change to real art is a swap rather than a
retune. Argue with them if the drawing wants different.

- The conduit must **touch the edge, not stop short of it**, and must be
  **centred on the edge midpoint**.
- The conduit's width **where it crosses the edge** should be **0.14 world
  units** — 16% of the tile's flat-to-flat width, or 28% of the centre-to-corner
  radius. This is the placeholder spoke width and reads clearly at phone size.
- That width is a **contract, not a suggestion**. Every tile of every kind, at
  every edge, must use the same mouth width, or joins will visibly step.
- The conduit may thicken, taper, braid, split or decorate **inside** the tile as
  much as you like. The contract applies only in a narrow band at the edge —
  say the outermost 8% of the radius. Keep the width constant across that band so
  a tiny sub-pixel misalignment never shows as a notch.
- Give yourself a **hard visual seam check**: two tiles side by side, each open on
  the facing edge, should look like one continuous conduit with no seam, no bulge
  and no colour step.
- The **centre of the tile** is where two or more conduits meet. Design a centre
  junction that works for every combination — two edges opposite each other (a
  straight run), two edges at 60 degrees (a tight bend), two at 120 degrees (a
  wide bend), and up to all six. A single circular or hexagonal hub at the centre
  that all arms plug into is the reliable solution.

### How many tile shapes exist

Counting sets of two or more open edges out of six, and treating rotations of the
same shape as one drawing, there are exactly **12 distinct tile shapes**:

| Open edges | Distinct shapes | Examples (as edge indices) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3 | straight `0,3`; wide bend `0,2`; tight bend `0,1` |
| 3 | 4 | `0,1,2`; `0,1,3`; `0,1,4`; `0,2,4` |
| 4 | 3 | `0,1,2,3`; `0,1,2,4`; `0,1,3,4` |
| 5 | 1 | `0,1,2,3,4` |
| 6 | 1 | `0,1,2,3,4,5` |

Twelve shapes across four resources is **48 tile images** if every tile is drawn
whole. Section 10 offers a cheaper route.

## 7. The four resource kinds

**Established** (`src/Pathweaver.Core/Tiles/ResourceKind.cs`, PRD 3.1).

| Kind | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Water** | The tutorial resource; biome 1 level 1 is water only. The PRD's Relic examples call these "River tiles", so river/aqueduct imagery is sanctioned. |
| **Wind** | |
| **Crystal** | |
| **Trade** | The one abstract resource — goods and commerce rather than a substance. |

Each kind flows only between **its own** springs and hubs, and never mixes. On a
crowded late-game board, several kinds will be threaded past each other in the
same few cells. A player must be able to tell, **at a glance and without
counting**, which conduit belongs to which kind. This is the single most
load-bearing readability requirement in the game.

## 8. Accessibility: a hard requirement

**This is a requirement, not a preference.** It is already acknowledged as a
known gap in the code (`BoardPalette.cs` carries a comment saying exactly this,
and there is an open accessibility task, issue #28). The PRD lists accessibility
enhancements as a shipping commitment in its Phase 4 roadmap.

**Colour must never be the only difference between two resource kinds.** Four
resources flowing through geometrically identical conduits, distinguished only by
hue, is precisely the situation in which a colour-blind player loses the game
board. Roughly one man in twelve has some form of red-green colour deficiency.

Each kind needs **at least two independent channels** of identity. Colour is one.
The second must be a difference in **silhouette, surface pattern, or motif** that
survives being printed in greyscale.

### Proposals for the second channel

These are suggestions to react to, not instructions:

| Kind | Possible silhouette / edge profile | Possible surface motif |
|---|---|---|
| **Water** | Smooth, rounded channel with soft parallel banks | Flowing striations along the direction of travel; ripple chevrons |
| **Wind** | Open, airy, slightly narrower; a hollow duct rather than a filled pipe | Dashed or broken-line texture; a gap running down the centre |
| **Crystal** | Hard-edged, faceted, angular; visibly *not* curved | Prismatic facet planes; small repeated diamond nodes |
| **Trade** | Segmented, jointed, mechanical; regular hard divisions along its length | Repeating tick marks or rungs, like a track or a rope with knots |

Notice that all four differ in **line quality** (soft / hollow / faceted /
segmented) before colour is applied at all. That is the property to aim for.

### Self-test before delivering

1. Desaturate the whole board to greyscale. Can you still name every conduit's
   kind? If not, the design fails.
2. Run it through a deuteranopia and a protanopia simulation. Same question.
3. Shrink it to 90 pixels tall — the smallest a tile gets on a phone with a large
   board, see section 10 — and ask again. Detail that only reads at 500 pixels is
   decoration, not information.

### The placeholder palette

**Reported from code.** `BoardPalette.cs` already picked colours specifically to
stay distinguishable under the common forms of colour blindness. The project
renders in **Gamma colour space**, so these values map straight to sRGB with no
gamma surprise — what you author is what appears.

| Purpose | sRGB hex | Unity float value |
|---|---|---|
| Water conduit | `#4099E6` | 0.25, 0.60, 0.90 |
| Wind conduit | `#99D9BF` | 0.60, 0.85, 0.75 |
| Crystal conduit | `#D973A6` | 0.85, 0.45, 0.65 |
| Trade conduit | `#E69940` | 0.90, 0.60, 0.25 |
| Empty cell | `#383D4A` | 0.22, 0.24, 0.29 |
| Placed conduit backing | `#525969` | 0.32, 0.35, 0.41 |
| Legal placement cell | `#4D5C70` | 0.30, 0.36, 0.44 |
| Harvest flash | `#F2F7FF` | 0.95, 0.97, 1.00 |
| Spring | `#F2D959` | 0.95, 0.85, 0.35 |
| Hub | `#8C66D9` | 0.55, 0.40, 0.85 |

Use it as a **starting point you are free to improve**, not a spec. It is
programmer art. But if you shift a hue, re-run the colour-blindness check
yourself — the current separation was deliberate and easy to destroy by accident.
Note also that the four conduit colours currently sit on a dark, low-saturation
board; contrast against `#383D4A` and `#525969` is part of what makes them read.

## 9. Cell states that need art

**Established** — enumerated from `Assets/Scripts/Presentation/CellView.cs`, which
is the only code that decides what a cell shows, plus `BoardPalette.cs`.

Every cell of the board is in exactly one of these states at any moment.

| # | State | What it means to the player | What the code does today | What is needed from you |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Empty cell** | Nothing here; you may or may not be able to build here | Flat hexagon in `#383D4A`, no spokes | The resting board surface. Quiet, low contrast — it is the majority of the screen and must not compete with conduits. |
| 2 | **Empty cell, legal for the held tile** | The tile in your hand can go *here* | Same hexagon in the lighter `#4D5C70` | A clearly readable "you may build here" treatment. Note the code comment: this is **not a hint, it is the placement rule made visible** — a tile must join the existing network, and without this signal a refused tap reads as the game ignoring the player. It must be unmistakable, yet calm enough to have several on screen at once. |
| 3 | **Placed conduit** | A tile you have laid | Hexagon in `#525969` plus one spoke per open edge in the kind colour | The tile art proper. Backing plus conduit, per sections 6 and 7. |
| 4 | **Conduit on a route that just paid out** | *That* is the route that scored | The whole route's cells flash to near-white `#F2F7FF` for **0.9 seconds**, conduits keeping their kind colour | The celebration. The whole route lights, not the last tile placed, because what paid out is the route and the player needs to see which one. See the proposal below. |
| 5 | **Spring** | A source; flow begins here | Hexagon in `#F2D959` with an east–west bar in the kind colour | A source. Must read as *emitting*. |
| 6 | **Hub** | A destination; flow is harvested here | Hexagon in `#8C66D9` with an east–west bar in the kind colour | A destination. Must read as *receiving*. |

**Now established in code:** springs and hubs animate, and the direction is the role. A ring
grows from the centre of a spring out to its rim; on a hub it collapses from the rim to the
centre. That is the "radiating versus converging silhouette" proposed below, built as
`EndpointPulse` — so the non-colour channel for source-versus-destination exists today, and
your art should assume it. The ring is a separate object drawn between the cell background
and the conduit arms, so it never covers the resource motif.

Additional established facts about springs and hubs:

- **A spring or hub occupies its own cell, and is never a conduit.** The player
  cannot place a tile on one. They are authored into the level, not drawn from
  the deck.
- **They have no facing.** Flow enters the network through *any* neighbouring
  conduit that is open towards the endpoint and carries the matching resource. A
  spring works identically on the board's rim and deep in its interior, so the
  art must look correct with conduits arriving from any of the six sides, or from
  only one.
- **They also carry a resource kind.** So a spring must communicate *three*
  things simultaneously: that it is an endpoint, that it is a source rather than a
  destination, and which of the four kinds it belongs to. The role/kind split in
  the placeholder — background colour for role, conduit colour for kind — is one
  workable answer, but the source-versus-destination distinction needs a
  non-colour channel too, for the same accessibility reason as section 8. A
  radiating versus converging silhouette is the obvious candidate.

### Proposal for the harvest flash

Rather than a second "lit" version of all 48 tiles, deliver **one additive glow
overlay** per kind (or one neutral one), sized to the hex, which the game
composites over the normal tile during the 0.9-second flash. That keeps the
texture count flat and lets the animation be tuned in code without redelivering
art. If the flash instead needs a genuinely different painting, say so now,
because it changes the budget arithmetic.

**Also now established:** a completed route carries a travelling light along the centre
channel of its conduits (`FlowPulse`). It is a small disc in the resource colour, brightened,
drawn in front of the arms — so the clear central channel described below is no longer a
future requirement but a present one.

The Supporter Pass's **golden path particle trails** (PRD 6.2) will also travel
along completed routes. They are particle effects rather than tile art, but the
conduit's interior needs a clear, unobstructed channel down its centre for
something to visibly move along. Design for that even though the trails
themselves are post-launch.

## 10. How big the art needs to be

This section is entirely **derived**: the numbers come from the camera code, and
the conclusions are **Proposals** you can push back on.

### How much of the screen a tile actually covers

`BoardCameraFitter.cs` frames the whole board on screen, reserving the bottom
**24% of the screen height** for the tile tray, plus a 0.25 world-unit margin
around the board. On a tall portrait phone the *width* runs out first, so the
board's width is what decides the zoom.

Working it through for a reference phone at **1080 × 2376** and the current
tutorial level (`biome1-01`, a hexagon-shaped board of radius 3): the board spans
5.196 × 4.5 world units, the camera settles at an orthographic size of 7.37, and
one world unit lands on **161 device pixels**.

A tile is 0.866 × 1.0 world units, so it covers roughly **140 × 161 device
pixels**.

| Board size | 1080 × 2376 phone | 1440 × 3120 phone | 1600 × 2560 tablet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hexagon radius 3 (tutorial) | 140 × 161 px | 186 × 215 px | 207 × 239 px |
| Hexagon radius 4 | 111 × 128 px | 148 × 171 px | 164 × 190 px |
| Hexagon radius 5 | 92 × 106 px | 123 × 142 px | 136 × 157 px |

Two things fall out of this table, and both matter more than the file format.

**First: tiles are small.** The realistic worst case is about **92 pixels wide**.
At 405 dpi that is under 9 millimetres — right at the minimum comfortable touch
target, and small enough that fine linework, thin outlines and subtle texture
simply disappear. Design for the 92-pixel case and let the tablet get a bonus,
not the other way round. If a design only reads at 200 pixels, it does not read.

**Second: the largest a tile ever gets is about 239 pixels.** Everything above
that is headroom, not visible detail.

### Proposed delivery specification

| Property | Proposed value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Format | **PNG, 8 bits per channel, RGBA with straight (unpremultiplied) alpha, non-interlaced** | Lossless, universally supported, and the alpha channel is required because tiles are not rectangular. Unity expects straight alpha; premultiplied alpha produces dark fringes. |
| Colour profile | **sRGB, or none** | The project renders in Gamma colour space. An embedded wide-gamut profile will shift the colours. |
| Canvas size, single tile | **512 × 512 px** | A power of two, which atlases and compresses cleanly. 512 gives just over 2× the largest on-screen size (239 px), so the art never has to be scaled *up*, and mipmaps handle the phone case cleanly. 256 would be visibly soft on a tablet; 1024 would quadruple the memory for detail nobody can see. |
| Pixels per unit | **512** | Chosen so one 512-pixel canvas equals exactly 1.0 world unit, which is exactly the hexagon's corner-to-corner height. That makes the mapping trivial: 1 world unit = 512 px, everywhere in the project. Set the same value on every board sprite so nothing is accidentally half-scale. |
| Hexagon size on that canvas | **512 px tall, 443.4 px wide** | The geometry from section 3: width is `0.866 × height`. Do not fill the canvas horizontally. |
| Pivot | **Centre — exactly (256, 256)** | The game positions and rotates tiles about the hexagon's centre. A pivot even one pixel off centre makes the tile wobble as it rotates and drift out of alignment with its neighbours. |
| Padding | **The canvas must stay symmetric about the centre.** The 34.3 px of empty space either side, horizontally, is your bleed area for glow or overhang. Never crop or asymmetrically pad. | Any padding that moves the hexagon's centre away from the canvas centre shifts the pivot, and every tile on the board slides. Deliver the same canvas size for every tile, trimmed of nothing. |
| Bleed | **Keep everything load-bearing inside the hexagon.** Soft glow may extend into the 34.3 px side margin; hard geometry may not. | Tiles butt up flush, so anything outside the hexagon overlaps a neighbour. |
| Mip maps | Leave enabled (an engine setting, not yours) | The art is delivered at 2–5× its on-screen size; without mipmaps it will shimmer when the board is panned. |
| Compression | ASTC (engine setting) | For reference only, so you know the art will be lossily compressed on device. Avoid designs that depend on razor-sharp single-pixel edges or subtle flat gradients, which are what block compression damages most. |
| Layered source | **Deliver alongside, outside the app folder** | PSD / Procreate / Affinity source lets the art be re-cut later without a repaint. It must not go into `Assets/` or it ships in the download. |

### Two ways to build the tiles — pick one

**Tier A (Proposal, recommended for the first delivery): composable parts.** Do
not draw 48 whole tiles. Instead draw, per kind, one **half-conduit "arm"**
running from the tile centre to one edge midpoint, plus one **centre junction**
piece. The game already works exactly this way — it rotates a single arm into all
six positions — so every one of the 12 shapes assembles itself from those two
pieces. That is **4 kinds × 2 pieces = 8 conduit sprites**, plus the cell
backgrounds and endpoints. Perfect joins are guaranteed, because there is only
one arm and it is drawn once.

The cost is that every straight, bend and junction is built from identical parts,
so the tiles will look systematic rather than hand-composed.

**Tier B: whole tiles.** 12 shapes × 4 kinds = 48 sprites, each individually
composed. Richer, more hand-painted, closer to the "hand-painted tile themes"
language in the PRD's Supporter Pass. Roughly six times the art, six times the
texture budget, and every one of the 48 has to hit the edge contract in section 6
independently.

**A sensible split:** Tier A for the free biomes and the MVP, Tier B reserved for
the five paid Supporter Pass themes where the extra craft is the product. Nothing
about this is decided — raise it before starting.

### Naming convention

**Proposal.** Lowercase, underscores, no spaces, no capitals, no version numbers
in the delivered filename (version the source file instead, not the shipped one).

```
hex_cell_empty.png
hex_cell_legal.png
hex_cell_backing.png

conduit_water_arm.png            Tier A: one arm, drawn pointing east (edge 0)
conduit_water_junction.png       Tier A: the centre piece

conduit_water_e03.png            Tier B: whole tile, open on edges 0 and 3
conduit_water_e012.png           Tier B: whole tile, open on edges 0, 1 and 2

endpoint_spring_water.png
endpoint_hub_water.png

fx_harvest_glow.png
```

For Tier B, `e` is followed by the open edge indices in **ascending order**, using
the numbering in section 4. `conduit_crystal_e0145.png` is a crystal tile open
east, south-east, north-west and north-east. Each shape is delivered in **one**
canonical rotation only — pick the rotation whose lowest edge index is 0, and let
the game turn it.

For Tier A, the arm is drawn **pointing east (edge 0)**, horizontally to the
right, because that is the orientation the game rotates from.

## 11. Budget: why the sizes are not generous

**Established from the PRD** (section 5.1 and 5.2) and reflected in `CLAUDE.md`
as hard constraints:

- The whole download must stay **under 85 MB**.
- Cold boot to an interactive main menu must be **under 1.5 seconds**.

Both are affected by art more than by anything else in the project — the game
logic is a few hundred kilobytes. Texture *count* drives boot time, because every
separate texture is a separate load and a separate draw call. Texture *size*
drives download size.

Rough arithmetic so you can sanity-check your own plan. A 4096 × 4096 texture
atlas compressed with ASTC costs roughly **4 to 6 MB** in the download, and holds
64 sprites at 512 × 512. So:

- **Tier A** (about 8 conduit sprites plus backgrounds, endpoints and effects,
  call it 24 sprites) fits in a **single 2048 × 2048 atlas**, at roughly 1 to
  1.5 MB. Comfortable.
- **Tier B** (48 conduit sprites plus the rest, call it 64) fills **one 4096
  atlas**, at 4 to 6 MB. Also affordable — once. Five Supporter Pass themes at
  Tier B would be 20 to 30 MB, which is a third of the entire budget and needs a
  conversation before anyone starts painting.

Everything must go into **sprite atlases** — a small number of large textures
rather than a large number of small ones. That is an engine-side job, not yours,
but it constrains you in one way worth knowing: **keep every board sprite on the
same power-of-two canvas size**, because mixed and non-power-of-two sizes pack
badly and waste atlas space. It also means you should not deliver a sprite far
larger than it needs to be "just in case" — atlas space is the scarce resource.

## 12. Checklist before you deliver

Geometry:

- [ ] Pointy-top hexagon: corner top and bottom, vertical edges left and right.
- [ ] Hexagon is 512 px tall and 443.4 px wide on a 512 × 512 canvas.
- [ ] Hexagon centre is exactly at the canvas centre; canvas is symmetric.
- [ ] Every conduit touches its edge midpoint, centred, at the same width on
      every tile and every kind.
- [ ] Two adjacent tiles placed side by side show one continuous conduit with no
      seam, bulge or colour step.

Rotation:

- [ ] The tile looks correct at all six 60-degree rotations. Actually rotate it
      and look, six times.
- [ ] No drop shadow. No directional highlight. No gravity-dependent detail. No
      text, numerals or arrows.

Readability:

- [ ] All four kinds distinguishable in greyscale.
- [ ] All four kinds distinguishable under deuteranopia and protanopia
      simulation.
- [ ] All four kinds distinguishable at 92 px wide.
- [ ] Spring reads as a source and hub as a destination without relying on
      colour, and both still show their kind.
- [ ] The "legal placement" cell state is unmistakable with a dozen on screen.

Delivery:

- [ ] PNG, RGBA, 8-bit, straight alpha, sRGB or no profile.
- [ ] Consistent canvas size across every board sprite.
- [ ] Filenames follow section 10.
- [ ] Layered source files delivered separately, outside the app folder.

## 13. Open questions to settle before painting

1. Tier A or Tier B (section 10)? This changes the amount of work by roughly six
   times and should be decided first.
2. Is the board seen straight down, or at a slight tilt? Nothing has been decided,
   and a tilt interacts badly with free rotation.
3. Does the harvest flash need painted "lit" variants, or is an additive overlay
   enough (section 9)?
4. What are the first three biomes, actually? The PRD names frozen rivers and
   volcanic vents as example *mechanics*, not as a biome list. Someone has to
   choose, and it may as well be you.
5. Do the empty cell and the placed-conduit backing differ per biome, or is the
   biome expressed only in the surrounding board frame? Per-biome cell art
   multiplies the atlas count by the number of biomes.

## Installing your art: no code required

The board reads its artwork from a single asset, `Assets/Settings/BoardTheme.asset`. Every
field on it is optional, and anything left empty falls back to the placeholder geometry the
game ships with today.

That is deliberate. Art arrives one piece at a time, and a design that switched from
"all placeholder" to "all sprites" would mean nothing could be seen in the game until the last
file existed. Instead you can drop in a single conduit arm, run the game, and see it in place
with everything else still generated.

### Steps

1. Put your PNGs anywhere under `Assets/`, for example `Assets/Art/Tiles/`.
2. Select each one and set **Texture Type: Sprite (2D and UI)**, **Pixels Per Unit** to the
   value in the delivery table below, and **Pivot: Center**.
3. Open `Assets/Settings/BoardTheme.asset`.
4. Assign the sprites. The fields are:

| Field | What it replaces | Empty means |
|---|---|---|
| Cell Background | the hexagon behind everything | generated hexagon |
| Legal Cell Overlay | the marker on a cell where the held tile may go | a lighter background colour |
| Spring | the source cell | generated hexagon plus six marks |
| Hub | the destination cell | generated hexagon plus two marks |
| Resources → Arm | the conduit painted across a tile, drawn once per open edge and rotated into place | a generated bar |
| Resources → Motif | the shape identifying the resource | a generated circle, triangle, diamond or hexagon |
| Resources → Colour | tints the placeholder geometry | the placeholder palette |

The **Resources** list has one entry per resource kind. Add four entries and set each `kind`
field, or add only the kinds you have finished.

### The arm is drawn once per open edge

One arm sprite serves all six edges: the game instantiates it once per open edge and rotates
it. So the arm should be drawn **pointing east**, running from the tile centre to the middle of
the eastern edge, and it must look correct when that same image is rotated by any multiple of
60 degrees. This is why the rotation constraints in section 5 matter — they are not advice, they
are what makes one sprite cover six directions.

### Colour is a fallback, not a tint

If you supply a sprite, its own colours are used and the palette colour is ignored. If you
supply only a colour, it tints the generated geometry. Leaving a colour as fully transparent
counts as "not set" rather than as "invisible", so an unfinished entry cannot accidentally hide
a resource.
